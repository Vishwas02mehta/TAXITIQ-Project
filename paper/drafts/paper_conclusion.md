# Paper Conclusion

We presented a controlled empirical evaluation of local generator models inside a frozen legal RAG pipeline. Using the 60-question IndiaLawRAG v2 corpus-grounded benchmark, we compared Qwen2.5 7B Instruct and Qwen3 8B under identical retrieved contexts and prompt structures. Our results show that Qwen3 8B achieved a success rate of 71.67% compared to 43.33% for Qwen2.5 7B. This difference of 28.33 percentage points is statistically significant (p = 0.000221) with a bootstrap 95% CI of 15.00 to 41.67 percentage points.

Our analysis reveals that the difference is heavily associated with supported-question refusals: Qwen2.5 refused to answer 50.91% of supported questions despite correct evidence being present in context, indicating a highly conservative response behavior that prioritizes avoiding false claims over maximizing answer coverage. This discrepancy may reflect differences in instruction following, evidence utilization, uncertainty handling, or refusal behavior; however, the present experiment does not isolate the underlying model mechanism. Conversely, Qwen3's higher coverage came with a minor grounding trade-off (1 citation and 1 numeric grounding failure, both on Question Q18), highlighting the need to evaluate answer coverage and grounding reliability jointly.
